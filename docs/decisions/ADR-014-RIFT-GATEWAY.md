# ADR-014 — A continuous spatial rift-to-gateway opening

2026-09-27. Explicitly authorized by “continue implement this”, following the refined plan. Supersedes ADR-013's image-based arrival. Scope: the opening and gateway, with the existing product overview as the exit.

One persistent R3F canvas renders the entire sequence. GSAP controls normalized native-scroll progress; CameraDirector remains the sole camera writer. Twenty-eight instanced mineral blocks share beveled geometry and a procedural surface, moving from orbiting fractured stone into architectural piers. A vertex shader straightens the same geometry during assembly. The camera advances through the energy plane before settling toward the assembled gateway. Thin orbit trails travel toward the overhead mechanism. Foreground stones spread alongside the approach. Particle spring targets become falling sparks.

GatewayWorld owns environmental architecture, suspended rings, stairs, pathway lights, energy column, distant cliff silhouettes, mist layers and shallow-water shaders. These are fictional environment assets, not product geometry. No physical cabinet was invented. A real Altitude focus/screen-entry journey still requires TP-001.

All shader resources have explicit ownership. Shader uniforms are updated on the mounted materials. Ambient motion uses the existing paused/offscreen-aware demand renderer. Mobile starts with independently centered stone coordinates and uses a more distant arrival camera. Scroll extent is 480svh desktop, 330svh mobile. Balanced/software profiles omit planar reflection and bloom; the floor uses a procedural illuminated reflection approximation. High quality retains the existing planar reflection. Source stills remain the loading/failure presentation, and a semantic gateway section accompanies the static fallback.

Optional synthesized ambient sound is muted by default, starts only after a user gesture, fades out when paused/offscreen, and closes its AudioContext on unmount. It is a minimal tonal atmosphere, not a produced soundtrack.

The render is a procedural interpretation of the two supplied reference images. It does not reproduce their fine photographic detail. No award, physical-device frame rate, or cross-browser fidelity is claimed.
